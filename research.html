Research

Learning scientific structure from data

My work combines mathematical analysis, computation, and data to discover governing mechanisms, solve differential equations, and make learning methods more reliable.

01

Learning dynamical systems and collective behavior

I develop nonparametric and structure-aware methods for learning how interacting agents and stochastic systems evolve. The goal is to infer interaction laws, hidden variables, and environmental effects from observations, then use those learned mechanisms to explain emergent behavior.

  • Interaction-kernel learning from trajectories and collective steady states
  • Clustering, flocking, swarming, synchronization, and social hierarchy
  • System identification for SDEs and stochastic PDEs
  • Applications in celestial mechanics, biological systems, and urban crime
02

Physics-informed and equation-based learning

I study training strategies and mathematical foundations for learning with differential equations. This includes physics-informed neural networks, Gaussian processes, random-feature methods, and domain-decomposition approaches designed for stability, efficiency, and interpretability.

  • Stable training for stiff and hyperbolic PDEs
  • Dimension-wise interpretability and adaptive domain decomposition
  • Uncertainty quantification and event-structured learning
  • Applications in power systems, supernova simulation, and conservation laws
03

Inverse problems, numerical analysis, and scientific computing

My broader work addresses recovery and modeling problems in which observations are incomplete or indirect. I develop analysis and computation together, using regularization and numerical structure to make inference more dependable.

  • Regularization for ill-posed linear inverse problems
  • Image registration and signal recovery
  • Finite-element and numerical PDE methods
  • Scientific computing, optimal transport, and high-dimensional computation
